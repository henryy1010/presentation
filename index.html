THE BIG IDEA01 / 08

Scaffolding Difficult Authentic Reading Texts for Low-Level Learners

Authentic texts show students how English is used in real life. However, unfamiliar words and long sentences can make these texts difficult for beginners.

Scaffolding gives learners clear, temporary support. It helps them understand the text, take part in the lesson, and read with more confidence.

This presentation explains how to support students before, during, and after reading.

TEMPORARY SUPPORT02 / 08

What Is Scaffolding in Reading?

Scaffolding is the temporary support a teacher provides when a task is too difficult for students to complete independently.

In reading lessons, this support may include explaining key words, showing pictures, dividing a text into sections, and asking guiding questions.

At first, the teacher gives more help. As students improve, the teacher gradually reduces this support.

The goal is to help learners move from supported reading to independent reading.

REAL-WORLD ENGLISH03 / 08

What Are Authentic Reading Texts?

Authentic texts are created for real communication, rather than specifically for language learners. They show how English is used outside the classroom.

  1. 01News articles, websites, and travel advertisements provide real information.
  2. 02Menus, emails, and messages help people communicate in everyday situations.
  3. 03Instructions and product descriptions help people understand what to do.

These materials connect classroom learning with real-life needs.

THE CHALLENGES04 / 08

Why Do Beginners Find Them Difficult?

Low-level learners may understand some words but still struggle to follow the whole text.

  1. 01Unfamiliar vocabulary makes important information difficult to understand.
  2. 02Long sentences and complex grammar make meaning harder to follow.
  3. 03Cultural references and too much information can cause confusion.
  4. 04Low confidence can reduce motivation and participation.

Teachers should identify these difficulties and choose suitable support.

BEFORE READING05 / 08

Pre-Reading Scaffolding Strategies

Before reading, the teacher prepares students for the topic and gives them a clear starting point.

  1. 01Activate prior knowledge. Ask what students already know about the topic.
  2. 02Pre-teach key vocabulary. Explain five or six essential words using simple definitions.
  3. 03Use visual support. Show relevant pictures to help students understand the context.
  4. 04Make predictions. Use the title and images to guess the content.

These activities make the text more familiar and help students feel prepared.

DURING READING06 / 08

While-Reading Scaffolding Strategies

During reading, students need to understand information step by step.

  1. 01Chunk the text: divide it into short, manageable sections.
  2. 02Read for gist: identify the general meaning before focusing on details.
  3. 03Use guiding questions: help students find the most important information.
  4. 04Highlight key information: underline useful words and main ideas.
  5. 05Use pair work: let students discuss difficult parts with a partner.

The focus is on understanding meaning, rather than translating every word.

A CLASSROOM EXAMPLE · A1–A207 / 08

Reading a Travel Advertisement

Students read a real advertisement for a weekend trip to London.

  1. 01Before reading, the teacher shows pictures of London and explains key words: ticket, hotel, price, and departure.
  2. 02During reading, students read short sections and answer three questions: Where is the trip? How much does it cost? When does it begin?
  3. 03After reading, students work in pairs. They decide whether they would like to take the trip and explain their choice in simple English.

The activity combines authentic reading, comprehension, and communication.

TOWARD INDEPENDENCE08 / 08

Conclusion: From Support to Independence

Scaffolding makes difficult authentic texts more accessible while keeping their real-world meaning.

It improves reading comprehension and vocabulary knowledge. It also builds confidence, increases motivation, and encourages active participation.

Teachers should give strong support at the beginning and gradually reduce it as students become more capable.

With effective scaffolding, learners move from reading with help to reading independently.
